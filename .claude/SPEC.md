# Nexo: game spec for the BGA adaptation

Source: official rulebook, English, `misc/Nexo_Rules (EN).pdf` (Marc Rebillet, Steffen Spiele; 4 pages EN). Items marked **[BGA]** are deliberate choices for the BGA version, either where the rules are silent or where we knowingly differ from the physical game. Mark anything confirmed with the publisher as **[BGA, confirmed with publisher]** and anything still open as **[BGA, pending publisher confirmation]**. Rule questions the rulebook left open, and their answers: `.claude/open-questions.md`.

## Glossary

- **Disc**: one wooden piece. Each player has 25 in their color, red or black.
- **Reserve**: a player's discs not on the board (`player.reserve`, starts at `DISCS_PER_PLAYER` 25).
- **Tower**: 1 to 5 discs of one color stacked on one spot (one `towers` row: `tower_x`, `tower_y`, `player_id`, `height`). Its **height** is its number of discs.
- **Spot**: a position of the invisible grid (`x`, `y`, unbounded integers, the first tower of the game at 0 0, y growing downwards).
- **Grid**: the invisible, dynamic 5x5 grid (`GRID_SIZE`), its bounds set by the furthest towers in each direction. A tower may not create a 6th row or a 6th column.
- **Turn**: a player uses 1 to 5 discs from their reserve (`MAX_DISCS_PER_TURN`), split into one or more towers (`PlaceTowers`, `actPlaceTowers`).
- **New tower**: a tower on an empty spot, orthogonally adjacent to a tower of any color (`kind` `new`, `PLACEMENT_NEW`).
- **Replacement**: a tower put on an enemy tower's spot, at least one disc higher; the enemy tower's discs go back to its owner's reserve (`kind` `replace`, `PLACEMENT_REPLACE`).
- **Pass**: a player with no valid move skips their turn (`NextPlayer`, `playerPassed`). **Passed**: their last turn was a pass (`player.passed`), until they place again.
- **Group** (rulebook: "area"): towers of one color connected orthogonally. A player's **score** is the number of towers in their largest group, whatever their heights (`getLargestGroups()`).
- **Draft** [BGA]: the turn being built on the client before it is confirmed (`PlaceTowers.ts`); its towers are **drafted towers** (`PendingTower`).
- **Legal spot** [BGA]: an empty spot where the active player may place a new tower now. **Replaceable tower**: an enemy tower they can out-height with the discs left this turn (highlighted and clickable like a legal spot).

## Components

- 50 wooden discs: 25 red, 25 black.
- No board: the grid is invisible.

## Setup

- 2 players only. Each takes the 25 discs of a color as their reserve.
- **[BGA]** Colors are BGA player colors: `ff0000` plays red, `000000` plays black (`gameinfos.jsonc` `player_colors`, `DISC_COLOR_BY_PLAYER_COLOR`). **[BGA]** The colors are random every game (`setupNewGame` shuffles them), whatever the seats; favourite colors are ignored (`favorite_colors_support` false, no `reattributeColorsBasedOnPreferences`). Red starts.
- The player with the red discs begins. **[BGA]** `setupNewGame` makes the red player active, whatever the seat order.

## Turn sequence

1. The active player must place a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 5 discs from their reserve, as 1 or more towers, each either:
   - a **new tower** on an empty spot, orthogonally adjacent to at least one tower of any color (except the very first tower of the game), or
   - a **replacement** of an enemy tower by a tower at least one disc higher; the replaced discs go back to the opponent's reserve. A replacement needs no adjacency check: every tower on the board except the first is adjacent to another tower and spots are never emptied, so an enemy tower is never on its own. Replacements never change the grid's bounds.
   - A tower is 1 to 5 discs high. Nobody ever adds discs on top of an existing tower, theirs or the opponent's. Only an enemy's tower can be replaced, so a spot taken earlier in the turn can't be replaced again: you never replace your own tower.
   - No tower may create a 6th row or a 6th column of the grid.
   - **[BGA]** The turn is checked as a whole: a new tower may be adjacent to any tower, including one placed earlier in the same turn (every new tower must reach the towers already on the board through the turn's other new towers), and the order the towers were drafted in doesn't matter.
   - **[BGA]** The very first tower of the game goes at 0 0 (coordinates are relative, so every spot of an empty board is the same); the server refuses a first turn without a tower there.
   - **[BGA] Draft then confirm**: the active player clicks or taps a highlighted spot (a dashed ellipse in their own disc color, its dashes slowly travelling around it (one trip in about 97s, 83s on a phone), still with reduced motion: an empty legal spot, or, a little wider and under the tower, an enemy tower they can replace, clickable like a legal spot). **Height by clicks**: the first click drafts a tower there at the lowest legal height (1 disc; for a replacement, the enemy height + 1). Each click on that drafted tower adds one disc; a click when it is already at the highest it may be (5 discs, or every disc left this turn) takes it back, and the next click starts again at the lowest. A turn may hold several towers, so a click on another highlighted spot starts a tower there and the others stay. **[BGA] Too high to replace**: a click on an enemy tower the player could replace but for the discs left this turn (it needs its height + 1, at most 5) changes nothing, but the tower gives a short shake (none with reduced motion) and a small bubble just above it, arrow pointing at it, says "Not enough [disc]: need 5, you have 3", the word "discs" being a small disc in the player's own color, the status bar's disc icon (the numbers from the same rule as the highlighted towers); it fades in, stays about 1.8s, fades out, never covers a click, stays on the board near its edges (under the tower when there is no room above), and a new click replaces it. A 5-disc enemy tower, which nothing can replace, and the player's own towers still do nothing. Only for the player drafting, on their page: nothing is sent or logged. A gold ring marks the drafted tower clicked last. Each drafted tower shows semi-transparent on its spot (a replaced enemy tower fades under it), with a small badge giving its number of discs. Only the **active tower**, the one clicked last (a new spot or a drafted tower), has the gold ring and, just under it, two buttons: a round undo button (a counter-clockwise arrow, at least 32px on screen) that takes it back, together with any new tower that no longer reaches the board through the others (on the first turn, the towers still connected to the earliest one left stay), and a green confirm button with a white tick, which sends every drafted tower of the turn; the server checks every rule again. A ring of thick segments around the confirm button, with a small gap between each, shows the turn's progress: one segment per disc the player may use this turn (5, fewer when their reserve is lower), solid green for each disc drafted, pale grey for the others. A segment that fills fades from grey to green with a slight pop (under 0.2s), and one emptied by an undo fades back the same way; when the buttons first appear, the segments already filled fill one after another, 50ms apart. Nothing loops; with reduced motion the ring just changes. While discs are left, the button is light green with a green tick and outline (confirming early is allowed, so it still looks clickable); once every disc is drafted, the ring is full and the button turns solid green with a white tick, with one short pulse. Both update at once as discs are drafted, cycled or undone. Once the active tower is taken back, by undo or by clicking past its highest, the tower clicked before it (still drafted) is active again, gold ring and buttons included; with no tower left, no buttons. Both buttons are the same size, side by side, centred under the tower and always wholly on the board (nudged sideways at its left and right edges, shown above the tower on the bottom row). **[BGA] Confirm anyway?** Confirming while discs can still be placed this turn (fewer drafted than the turn allows, and somewhere to put another: a legal spot, a replaceable tower, or a drafted tower one more click would raise) doesn't send the turn: a small card opens just under the confirm button (above it when there is no room below), with an arrow pointing at it: "You haven't placed all your discs. Confirm anyway?", with two buttons on one line: "Cancel" on the left (closes it) and "Confirm" on the right (green, sends the turn). It fades in quickly (none with reduced motion), stays whole on screen and inside the game area, sits over the board and the status bar, and never moves the page. It closes on "Cancel", a click or tap outside it, Escape, and any change to the draft; a second press of the confirm button while it is open confirms. With every disc drafted, or none placeable any more, the confirm button sends at once. Confirm is sent once: both buttons grey out until the server answers, and come back if it refuses the turn. **[BGA]** There are no buttons in the status bar for this move (no Confirm, no "Reset turn": undo each tower instead), so the board never moves while drafting; the status bar keeps one title for the whole draft, "You must place up to" followed by one small disc icon per disc the turn may use (5, fewer when the reserve is lower; a single icon when it is 1), drawn like the board's discs in the player's color and sized to the text line. Each drafted disc dims one icon, from the right, so the full ones stay next to the text, and an undo brightens the leftmost dimmed one first; icons are never added or removed, so the title never changes width. The opponent and spectators see "[name] must place up to" with the same number of icons, always full (they don't see the draft). Legal spots and replaceable towers are recomputed after every change (adjacency and the grid's bounds both change as you draft). A reload loses the draft (it lives on the page only).
   - **[BGA] Board view**: the board shows only the towers (and, to the player placing, the legal spots around them): no grid dots, no frame, no other space. It starts small around the first tower and stays centred on the bounding box of the towers plus the legal spots: whenever that box changes at the end of a turn (once its towers have landed, the player's own confirmed turn included), the contents slide smoothly (about 0.3s, easing out) to the new centre, never jump; on load there is no slide. **[BGA]** While the active player drafts, the board stays where it was when the turn began, even if the draft looks off-centre: drafting, cycling, undo and changing the active tower never move it. The new legal spots their drafted towers open show at once (they may be built on this turn). Only if the draft would reach past the board's edge (a line of 4 or 5 new towers from the edge) does it slide, by just enough to keep everything on the board. Towers never move relative to each other: only the whole contents move together. **[BGA]** On the board, a disc is 0.86 of a spot wide (0.87 on a phone), so neighbouring towers stand close together with a small, clear gap. An empty target ring is exactly the footprint of the disc that would go there; the ring around a replaceable enemy tower is the same ellipse a little larger (1.12 times), showing evenly around its base, and never touches a neighbouring ring. Both players, spectators, a reload and a replay show the same layout. Legal spots show only to the active player while they place, as dashed ellipses in their own disc color (red or black); the passive player, spectators and replays never see them, so nothing looks clickable when it isn't. They still count in the box for everyone, so the board sits in the same place on every screen and doesn't slide when the turn passes. The board is 6.7 x 6.7 spots on desktop, room for the largest possible box (6 x 6) and an edge of a third of a spot on each side, so the outer targets have room around them; 6.3 x 6.3 on a phone, a thinner edge (7 x 7 until 2026-11-18), and as big as the window allows: square, about 10px under the status bar and 10px above the window's bottom (with the page scrolled to its top; BGA's settings come below it, after the gap below), never wider than the game area; it resizes with the window. No panel above the board.
   - **[BGA] Gap under the board**: under the board, only the page's background shows for 40% of the window's height (50% on a phone, BGA's mobile layout) before BGA's own content after the game (footer, menus, links, table info). With the page at its top, that content is never on screen, even on a tall phone where the board is short: the gap grows to push it under the screen's bottom. The gap is empty (nothing in it reacts to a click or a tap). BGA's top bar, the status bar and the board stay at the top of the page as before.
2. **Pass**: there is no voluntary pass: a player with a valid move must place. A player who can't make a valid move must pass: no discs left in their reserve, or discs but nowhere legal to put them (no legal empty spot, and no enemy tower they can out-height with at most min(5, reserve) discs, so a player whose only moves would need more than 5 discs, or more than their reserve, passes).
   - **[BGA]** Passing is automatic: the server passes for them (`NextPlayer`), logs it (the player's name and a ban sign, which reads "No valid move, passes" on hover, like Riviera's skipped power) and shows a PASSED stamp on their player board until they place again. The stamp shows only while exactly one player is passed: both passed means the game is over, so neither board shows it then, nor once the game has ended (after a reload too). Every pass, the player's own included, first shows a big PASSED stamp with the player's name at the centre of the screen: it holds briefly, then shrinks onto that player board's stamp and becomes it, or fades out there on the pass that ends the game; the next pass or the game end waits for it (about 1.5s). No banner with animations off, in fast replay or in a replay. A passed player is back in the game as soon as they have a valid move (discs returned by a replacement, or a spot opening up).
3. The turn goes to the other player.
   - **[BGA]** Client: the other player's (and every spectator's) view of a turn: the turn plays one spot at a time, in the order it was drafted: on each spot, the replaced tower (if any) flies back onto its owner's reserve stacks on the player board, top disc first, each disc landing on top of the leftmost stack with room, while the new tower's discs fly from the top of the mover's rightmost non-empty stack onto the spot, bottom disc first, leaving 50ms apart, and the next spot's discs start leaving 30% of the way through this spot's flight. Then the boards that got discs back bounce, scores update, then the board re-centres (a smooth slide). The active player sees the same flights for their own turn: their drafted towers stay as placeholders until their spot's turn, then fly in from their stacks, each placeholder disc staying on the spot until its solid disc lands on it. No flights on F5, in fast replay or with animations off.
   - **[BGA]** Reserve on the player boards: the discs themselves, as 5 stacks of up to 5 side by side in the player's color, filled left to right (a reserve of 12: 5, 5, 2, 0, 0). Discs leave from the top of the rightmost stack that has any and come back onto the top of the leftmost stack with room, so the stacks empty right to left and refill left to right. An empty stack shows a faint outline. No number: a tooltip on the stacks gives the exact count ("N discs left"). Always rebuilt from the server's count on load and after every turn and pass, so it never drifts.
   - **[BGA]** Game log: each turn's row shows the player's name and, on its right, a small snapshot of the board as it stood after the turn, cropped to the towers (a 2 x 3 board gives a 2 x 3 snapshot, at most 5 x 5), every tower at its height and color, a faint dot on empty spots inside it, and a gold ring on every spot the turn touched (new towers and replacements). Its spots have a fixed size (a bigger board, a bigger snapshot), scaled down only when the log is too narrow. The same after a reload and in a replay.

## Scoring and game end

- The game ends when both players pass consecutively (`PASSES_TO_END_GAME` 2, global `consecutivePasses`, reset by every turn with a placement).
- Each player scores 1 point per tower in their single largest group of orthogonally connected towers of their color; height doesn't matter. Two groups tied for largest score that size. Most points wins; a tie shares the victory (equal score, no tiebreaker, `tie_breaker_description` empty).
- **[BGA]** Live score: BGA's score counter on each player board always shows the current largest group, recomputed for both players after every turn (a replacement can split the opponent's group).
- **[BGA]** End-game display: every largest group of each player glows under its towers (several when two groups tie for largest), every other tower dims. A log row shows the result: "name wins" (the name in their color, bold) over the score, "13-9" (the winner's largest group, then the other player's), a little larger and bolder, centred, with a charcoal trophy at each edge of the row, on a black-and-white marble background with the winner's color bar; a draw has a handshake at each edge and "Draw" over the tied score ("11-11"), on a marble going evenly from light to dark grey. Restored after F5 (`getAllDatas` `endGameScoring`).
- **[BGA]** Game progression: half the share of the 25 spots taken, half the share of the 50 discs on the board (discs on the board only ever go up: a replacement always puts down more than it gives back, so the game always ends), capped at 99 until the end.

## Zombie mode [BGA]

Level 1 (report it in the Game Metadata Manager): every choice is random among the actions a real player could take there (BGA's level 1: "a random possible action", `getRandomZombieChoice`).
- Place towers: like a real player's clicks, a random spot among every legal empty spot and every replaceable enemy tower, then a random height among the ones its clicks can reach, and the turn is confirmed with that single tower.
- Passing is automatic for everyone, zombies included.

## Game options [BGA]

None.

## Preferences [BGA]

- **Always show stack heights** (pref 100, `show_stack_heights`): Enabled (1, default) / Disabled (2). Disabled hides the height number on top of every tower: on the board, the flying towers and the log rows (a drafted tower's count badge stays). Display only, applied at once (no reload).

## Hidden information

None: the board, both reserves and the scores are public, to spectators too. The draft is local to the active player's page until confirmed.

## BGA states

Registered like Riviera's: one class per state in `modules/php/States/`, each with its `id:` in its constructor, and nothing else (no `states.inc.php`, no `$machinestates` array; see CLAUDE.md > Deleting or renaming PHP files). Ids 1 (setup) and 99 (game end) are BGA's own: no class uses them. Every transition is a returned class name, or `ST_END_GAME` (99) from `EndScore`. Only `PlaceTowers` and BGA's `gameEnd` have a client state class (`bga.states.register`); GAME states need none, as in Riviera.

| Id | Class | Type | Comes from | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (BGA setup) | | | `setupNewGame` returns `PlaceTowers` (10), red active |
| 10 | `PlaceTowers` | ACTIVE_PLAYER | setup; `NextPlayer` | `actPlaceTowers` (and the zombie, through it) → `NextPlayer` (90) |
| 90 | `NextPlayer` | GAME, updates progression | `PlaceTowers` | the next player has a valid move → `PlaceTowers` (10); else an automatic pass and the same check for the player after; the 2nd pass in a row → `EndScore` (98) |
| 98 | `EndScore` | GAME | `NextPlayer` | `ST_END_GAME` (99) |
| 99 | (BGA game end, client `gameEnd`) | | `EndScore` | |

- `PlaceTowers`: the active player drafts and confirms a turn (`actPlaceTowers`, a JSON list of `{x, y, height}`). Only reached with a valid move, so no pass action.
- `NextPlayer`: activates the other player and passes for anyone with no valid move (at most `PASSES_TO_END_GAME` loops).
- `EndScore`: final scores, stats, `displayEndGameScoring` (largest groups, winner log row).
- Debug jumps (Studio only): `debug_goToState($state = 10)`; `debug_setReserves()` and `debug_fillBoard()` → 10; `debug_endGame()` → 98.
- Client-only console helpers (no server call, no game state): `gameui.debug_passAnim(who)` plays the pass display exactly as `playerPassed` does (`who`: a turn-order position 1 to 4 by `player_no`, or a player id; returns a Promise), `gameui.debug_clearPassed()` hides every PASSED stamp, `gameui.debug_unaffordableTower(x, y)` clicks an enemy tower too high for the discs left (the first one without coordinates; only while drafting).
